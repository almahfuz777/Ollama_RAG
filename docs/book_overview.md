* Physical quantities and units.

1. **Physical Quantities**:  
   * Scalars: Quantities with only magnitude (e.g., mass, time).

5. Conversion of Units:  
   $$1 \, \text{km} = 1000 \, \text{m}, \, 1 \, \text{cm} = 0.01 \, \text{m}$$  

6. Area of Rectangle:  
   $$A = L \cdot B$$  

7. Volume of Rectangular Block:  
   $$V = L \cdot B \cdot H$$

# **Chapter 2: Motion**

* **Scope**: Study of rest, motion, and types of motion.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Scalars and vectors.  
  * Uniform and non-uniform motion.  
  * Equations of motion.  
  * Graphical representation of motion.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Types of Motion**:  
   - **Uniform**: Constant velocity.  
   - **Non-uniform**: Changing velocity.  

2. **Equations of Motion**: Derived for uniformly accelerated motion:  
   1. Velocity:  
      $$v = u + at$$  
   2. Displacement:  
      $$s = ut + \frac{1}{2} a t^2$$  
   3. Final Velocity:  
      $$v^2 = u^2 + 2as$$  

3. **Graphical Representation**:  
   - **Position-time graph**:  
     The slope of the graph represents velocity.  
   - **Velocity-time graph**:  
     - Slope = acceleration.  
     - Area under the graph = displacement.

**Key Formulas**:

1. Acceleration:  
   $$a = \frac{v - u}{t}$$  

2. Displacement for the nth second:  
   $$s_n = u + \frac{1}{2} a (2n - 1)$$  


**Key Formulas**:

1. Distance:  
   $$d = v \cdot t$$  

2. Velocity:  
   $$v = u + a \cdot t$$  

3. Displacement:  
   $$s = ut + \frac{1}{2} a t^2$$  

4. Final Velocity (without time):  
   $$v^2 = u^2 + 2a s$$  

5. Acceleration:  
   $$a = \frac{v - u}{t}$$  

6. Uniform Circular Motion:  
   $$v = \frac{2 \pi r}{T}$$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* Scalar quantities have magnitude only; vector quantities have magnitude and direction.  
* Motion is always relative to a reference frame.  
* Relative motion depends on the observer's frame of reference.  
- Circular motion involves centripetal acceleration:  
   $$a_c = \frac{v^2}{r}$$  


# **Chapter 3: Force**

* **Scope**: Concept of force and its effects on objects.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Newton's laws of motion.  
  * Momentum and impulse.  
  * Friction and its types.

**Key Formulas**:

1. Force:  
   $$F = m \cdot a$$  

2. Momentum:  
   $$p = m \cdot v$$  

3. Impulse:  
   $$J = F \cdot t$$  

4. Frictional Force:  
   $$F_f = \mu \cdot N$$  

5. Work:  
   $$W = F \cdot d \cdot \cos\theta$$  


**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Newton’s Laws of Motion**:  
   - **First Law**: Inertia.  
   - **Second Law**:  
     $$ F = m \cdot a $$  
   - **Third Law**:  
     Action-reaction pair:  
     $$ F_{action} = - F_{reaction} $$  

2. **Friction**:  
   - **Opposes motion.**  
   - **Static Friction**: Prevents motion.  
     $$ F_s \leq \mu_s \cdot N $$  
   - **Kinetic Friction**: Opposes motion in progress.  
     $$ F_k = \mu_k \cdot N $$  

3. **Momentum**:  
   - **Defined as**:  
     $$ p = m \cdot v $$  
   - **Impulse**: Change in momentum.  
     $$ J = F \cdot \Delta t $$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* Newton's 1st law: An object remains in rest or motion unless acted upon by an external force.  
* Momentum is conserved in isolated systems.


# **Chapter 4: Work, Power, and Energy**

* **Scope**: Mechanical work, power, and types of energy.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Work-energy theorem.  
  * Kinetic and potential energy.  
  * Conservation of energy.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Work**:  
   - Work is done when a force causes displacement.  
   - **Formula**:  
     $$ W = F \cdot d \cdot \cos \theta $$  

2. **Energy**:  
   - **Kinetic Energy**:  
     $$ KE = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 $$  
   - **Potential Energy**:  
     $$ PE = m g h $$  

3. **Power**:  
   - **Rate of doing work.**  
   - **Formula**:  
     $$ P = \frac{W}{t} $$  


**Key Formulas**:

1. **Work**:  
   $$ W = F \cdot d \cdot \cos \theta $$  

2. **Kinetic Energy**:  
   $$ KE = \frac{1}{2} m v^2 $$  

3. **Potential Energy**:  
   $$ PE = m \cdot g \cdot h $$  

4. **Power**:  
   $$ P = \frac{W}{t} $$  

5. **Efficiency**:  
   $$ \eta = \frac{\text{Output Energy}}{\text{Input Energy}} \times 100\% $$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* Work is done only if there is displacement in the direction of the force.  
* Energy can neither be created nor destroyed (Law of Conservation of Energy).

# **Chapter 5: Pressure and States of Matter**

* **Scope**: Study of pressure in fluids and properties of matter.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Pressure in liquids and gases.  
  * Pascal's principle and Archimedes' principle.  
  * Surface tension and viscosity.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Pressure**:  
- **Pressure**:  
   - Defined as:  
     $$ P = \frac{F}{A} $$  

   - **Fluid Pressure**:  
     $$ P = \rho g h $$  

2. **Pascal’s Law**:  
   * Pressure applied to an enclosed fluid is transmitted equally in all directions.  
3. **Archimedes’ Principle**:  
   * A body submerged in a fluid experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of the displaced fluid.  
4. **Viscosity and Surface Tension**:  
   * Viscosity: Resistance to flow.  
   * Surface tension: Cohesion of liquid molecules at the surface.

**Key Formulas**:

1. **Pressure**:  
   $$ P = \frac{F}{A} $$  

2. **Buoyant Force**:  
   $$ F_b = \rho \cdot V \cdot g $$  

3. **Fluid Pressure**:  
   $$ P = \rho \cdot g \cdot h $$  

4. **Surface Tension**:  
   $$ T = \frac{F}{L} $$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* Archimedes' principle: A body submerged in a fluid experiences an upward force equal to the weight of the displaced fluid.  
* Pascal's principle is the basis of hydraulic systems.

# **Chapter 6: Effect of Heat on Substances**

* **Scope**: Study of thermal properties of matter.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Temperature and heat.  
  * Expansion of solids, liquids, and gases.  
  * Specific heat capacity and latent heat.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Heat and Temperature**:  
   * Heat: Energy transfer due to temperature difference.  
   * Temperature: Measure of thermal energy.  
2. **Expansion**:  
   - **Linear Expansion**:  
     $$ \Delta L = \alpha L_0 \Delta T $$  

   - **Volume Expansion**:  
     $$ \Delta V = \beta V_0 \Delta T $$  

3. **Specific Heat and Latent Heat**:  
   - **Specific Heat (\( c \))**:  
     Energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by \( 1^\circ C \).  

   - **Latent Heat (\( L \))**:  
     Energy required to change the state of a substance without a change in temperature.  

**Key Formulas**:

1. Heat Energy:  
   $$ Q = m \cdot c \cdot \Delta T $$  

2. Thermal Expansion:  
   $$ \Delta L = \alpha \cdot L_0 \cdot \Delta T $$  

3. Latent Heat:  
   $$ Q = m \cdot L $$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* Heat flows from higher to lower temperature regions.  
* Specific heat is the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of substance by 1°C.  
* Metals expand when heated; this is used in thermometers.  
* Phase changes occur at constant temperature (e.g., ice melting at 0°C).

# **Chapter 7: Oscillations and Waves**

* **Scope**: Study of oscillatory motion and wave phenomena.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Simple harmonic motion (SHM).  
  * Damped and forced oscillations.  
  * Wave motion: transverse and longitudinal waves.  
  * Sound waves and resonance.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM)**:  
   * SHM is a type of oscillatory motion where the restoring force is directly proportional to displacement and acts towards the mean position.  
   * Examples: Pendulum, spring-mass system.

**Key Equations**:

1. Displacement:  
   $$ x(t) = A \sin(\omega t + \phi) $$  

2. Velocity:  
   $$ v(t) = \frac{dx}{dt} = A \omega \cos(\omega t + \phi) $$  

3. Acceleration:  
   $$ a(t) = -\omega^2 x $$  

4. Angular Frequency (for spring system):  
   $$ \omega = \sqrt{\frac{k}{m}} $$  


2. **Wave Motion:**  
   * Waves are disturbances that transfer energy through a medium or space.  
   * **Transverse waves**: Particles oscillate perpendicular to wave propagation (e.g., light waves).  
   * **Longitudinal waves**: Particles oscillate parallel to wave propagation (e.g., sound waves).  
3. **Sound Waves:**  
   * Longitudinal waves that propagate through compression and rarefaction.  
   * Resonance: When the frequency of an external force matches the natural frequency of a system, leading to large amplitude oscillations.

**Key Concepts**:

* Speed of sound in air: v=343 m/sv \= 343 \\, \\text{m/s}v=343m/s at 20∘ C20^\\circ \\, \\text{C}20∘C.  
* Doppler Effect: Change in frequency due to relative motion between source and observer.

**Equations**:

Doppler Effect:

1.  
   $$ f' = f \cdot \frac{v + v_o}{v + v_s} $$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* SHM is the foundation for understanding oscillatory and wave phenomena.  
* Wave interference can lead to constructive or destructive patterns.


# **Chapter 8: Electricity and Magnetism**

* **Scope**: Study of electrical and magnetic phenomena.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Electric charge, field, and potential.  
  * Current electricity: Ohm's Law and resistance.  
  * Magnetic fields and electromagnetic induction.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Electric Charge and Field**:  
   * Charge (q) is a fundamental property of matter.  
   * Electric field (E) is the force per unit charge exerted on a test charge in a field.

   **Key Equations**:

1. Coulomb’s Law:  
   $$ F = k \cdot \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} $$  

2. Electric Field:  
   $$ E = \frac{F}{q} = k \cdot \frac{q}{r^2} $$  


2. **Electric Potential**:  
   * Work done in moving a charge in an electric field. **Equation**:

   $$ V = k \cdot \frac{q}{r} $$  


3. **Current Electricity**:  
   * Flow of electric charges through a conductor.  
   * **Ohm’s Law**: V=IRV \= IRV=IR.  
   * Power in an electric circuit: P=IVP \= IVP=IV.  
4. **Magnetism**:  
   * Magnetic fields (BBB) are generated by moving charges or currents.  
   * Electromagnetic induction: A changing magnetic field induces an electric current.

**Key Equations**:

1. Biot-Savart Law:  
   $$ B = \frac{\mu_0}{4\pi} \cdot \frac{I \cdot dl \cdot \sin\theta}{r^2} $$  

2. Faraday’s Law:  
   $$ \text{EMF} = -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt} $$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* Electric and magnetic fields are interconnected (Maxwell's equations).  
* Practical applications: Generators, transformers, and electric motors.


# **Chapter 9: Thermodynamics**

* **Scope**: Study of heat, work, and energy transformations.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Laws of thermodynamics.  
  * Heat engines and refrigerators.  
  * Entropy and the Carnot cycle.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **First Law of Thermodynamics**:  
   - Energy conservation principle.  
     **Equation**:  
     $$ \Delta U = Q - W $$  

2. **Second Law of Thermodynamics**:  
   - Heat flows naturally from hot to cold bodies.  

3. **Carnot Engine**:  
   - Idealized heat engine with maximum efficiency.  
     **Efficiency**:  
     $$ \eta = 1 - \frac{T_c}{T_h} $$  

4. **Entropy**:  
   - Measure of disorder in a system.  
     **Equation**:  
     $$ \Delta S = \frac{Q}{T} $$  

**Relevant Notes**:

* Thermodynamics underpins engines, refrigerators, and power plants.

# **Chapter 11: Optics**

* **Scope**: Study of light, reflection, refraction, and optical instruments.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Reflection and refraction of light.  
  * Lenses and mirrors.  
  * Optical instruments: microscopes, telescopes.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Reflection**:  
   - Light bouncing off a surface.  
     **Key Laws:**  
     1. **Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection:**  
        $$ \theta_i = \theta_r $$  

2. **Refraction**:  
   - Bending of light as it passes from one medium to another.  
     **Key Equation:**  

- **Snell’s Law:**  
  $$ n_1 \sin \theta_1 = n_2 \sin \theta_2 $$  


3. **Lenses and Mirrors**:  
   - **Lenses form images by refraction, mirrors by reflection.**  
     **Equations:**  

   1. **Lens Formula:**  
      $$ \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u} $$  

   2. **Mirror Formula:**  
      $$ \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u} $$  

4. **Optical Instruments**:  
   * Microscope: Uses two lenses for magnification.  
   * Telescope: Used to observe distant objects.

**Key Formula**:

- **Magnification:**  
  $$ M = \frac{\text{Height of Image}}{\text{Height of Object}} $$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* Total internal reflection occurs when light travels from a denser to a rarer medium at an angle greater than the critical angle.  
* Applications: Fiber optics, periscopes.

# **Chapter 12: Gravitation**

* **Scope**: Study of gravitational forces and planetary motion.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Newton’s law of gravitation.  
  * Gravitational potential energy.  
  * Kepler’s laws of planetary motion.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Newton’s Law of Gravitation**:  
   - Every mass attracts every other mass with a force proportional to their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance.  
     **Equation:**  
     $$ F = G \cdot \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2} $$  

2. **Gravitational Potential Energy**:  
   - Energy due to position in a gravitational field.  
     **Equation:**  
     $$ U = -G \cdot \frac{m_1 m_2}{r} $$  

3. **Kepler’s Laws**:  
   - **First Law:** Planets move in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus.  
   - **Second Law:** A line joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal times.  
   - **Third Law:**  
     $$ \frac{T^2}{r^3} = \text{constant} $$  


**Relevant Notes**:

* Gravitational force governs celestial motion.  
* Satellites orbit Earth due to a balance between gravitational force and centripetal force.

# **Chapter 13: Properties of Solids and Liquids**

* **Scope**: Mechanical properties of solids and liquids.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Elasticity and stress-strain.  
  * Viscosity and surface tension.  
  * Fluid dynamics: Bernoulli’s principle.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Elasticity**:  
   - Ability of a material to regain its shape after deformation.  
     **Key Equation:**  

     **Stress:**  
     $$ \sigma = \frac{F}{A} $$  

     **Strain:**  
     $$ \epsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L} $$  

     **Young’s Modulus:**  
     $$ Y = \frac{\sigma}{\epsilon} $$  

2. **Viscosity**:  
   - Resistance to flow in fluids.  
     **Equation:**  
     $$ \eta = \frac{F}{A \cdot \frac{dv}{dx}} $$  

3. **Bernoulli’s Principle**:  
   - For incompressible, non-viscous flow, total mechanical energy remains constant.  
     **Equation:**  
     $$ P + \frac{1}{2} \rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant} $$  

**Relevant Notes**:

* Applications: Hydraulics, capillary action, and material science.  
* Surface tension explains phenomena like water droplets forming spheres.


# **Chapter 14: Electromagnetic Waves**

* **Scope**: Study of electromagnetic radiation and its properties.  
* **Key Topics**:  
  * Wave nature of electromagnetic radiation.  
  * Spectrum of electromagnetic waves.  
  * Applications of EM waves.

**Detailed Explanation**:

1. **Wave Nature**:  
   * Electromagnetic waves consist of oscillating electric and magnetic fields perpendicular to each other and to the direction of propagation.  
2. **Electromagnetic Spectrum**:  
   * Includes radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, and gamma rays. **Key Wavelength Ranges**:  
1. **Radio waves:**  
   $$ > 1 \, \text{m} $$  

2. **Visible Light:**  
   $$ 400 \, \text{nm} - 700 \, \text{nm} $$  

3. **X-rays:**  
   $$ 0.01 \, \text{nm} - 10 \, \text{nm} $$  

3. **Energy of a Photon**:  
   * E=hf, where hhh is Planck's constant and fff is the frequency.

**Relevant Notes**:

- **Applications:**  
  - Communication (radio, TV),  
  - Medical imaging (X-rays),  
  - Thermal imaging (infrared).  

- **Speed of Light:**  
  Electromagnetic waves propagate in a vacuum at the speed of light:  
  $$ c = 3 \times 10^8 \, \text{m/s} $$
